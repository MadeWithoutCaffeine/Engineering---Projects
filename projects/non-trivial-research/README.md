## Background

Most of my projects begin with something I want to build or understand. For this, I want to spend more time asking what problem is worth working on, what is already known, and whether the results could be useful to someone else.

I'm especially interested in aerospace and engineering, but I haven't committed to a research topic. I have a few possible directions in mind and want to wait until I know whether I'm accepted before choosing one.

## Current status

I'm working on applying. I haven't been accepted or started a Non-Trivial research project.

A friend is applying too. If one of us gets accepted, our plan is to help the person who does and contribute to the work together. I want us both to have a chance to learn from the project, even if only one application is successful. We would keep track of who contributes what and work within the program's participation arrangements.

## Possible directions

I have several engineering interests that could give me a starting point: flight efficiency, aircraft testing, lightweight structures, and collecting better measurements. My [project ideas](../project-ideas/README.md) include a wind tunnel, structure comparisons, and a lightweight FPV drone.

These are starting points for thinking, rather than a selected Non-Trivial proposal. Before choosing a direction, I would want to identify a specific problem, read existing work, and decide what I could realistically contribute with the time, tools, and support available.
